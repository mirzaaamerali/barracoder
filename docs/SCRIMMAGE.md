# Scrimmage readiness — Sun Nov 8, 2026

The scrimmage is a full dry run of the December main event: robot game matches on a competition table plus judging practice. Everything below derives from the [Robot Game Rulebook](../season/guides/fll-challenge-bioglow-rgr.pdf) and the Team Meeting Guide's judging expectations.

## Ready when

- [ ] Match format drilled: full 2.5-minute autonomous matches on a timer, run best-of-three style (only the best score counts; ties broken by 2nd then 3rd best) — team has run at least four rehearsal meetings of complete timed matches before Nov 8.
- [ ] Equipment inspection ready: robot + ALL equipment fits within the two launch areas under 12 in (305 mm); one-launch-area packing layout rehearsed for the 20-point inspection bonus; team can identify their self-built M13 keystone species model (bag 20 bricks, counts as equipment) to inspectors.
- [ ] Pre-match setup window rehearsed to a couple of minutes: distribute loose mission models and equipment between the two home areas, place robot in the chosen launch area, calibrate sensors on the mat, choose M13/M14/M15 dock placement (mine/city/farm), and ask the referee to check the field BEFORE the match.
- [ ] Technician discipline fixed: two technicians per home area, left/right groups locked with no side switching (same-side swaps only), everyone else standing back; handling of robot/equipment only when completely within your own home area.
- [ ] Precision-token rules internalized: six tokens (50 pts) to start; one lost per interruption outside home; end-of-match values 1=10/2=15/3=25/4=35/5=50/6=50; the free final stop-in-place exception; relaunch rules (partly outside = same home area, completely outside = either).
- [ ] High-cost rules mastered via recurring quizzes: M04 katydid even partly outside the leaf habitat = zero for the mission; no separating Dual Lock or breaking mission models; robot/model combinations must free in one motion in perfect condition; objects obtained outside home after the last launch go to the referee; points scored by hand outside home don't count.
- [ ] Variability-proofed programs: M13-15 mission programs work in all three dock assignments; runs don't depend on M04 katydid/leaf placement (referee randomizes every match) or a fixed M02 tube orientation; robot launches from a consistent, noted position and the whole robot fits inside the launch area.
- [ ] Mission strategy + contingency locked: program run order decided, mission owners and robot operators assigned, attachments in labeled per-mission bags and easy to grab, alternate-mission contingency plan for mid-match failures rehearsed.
- [ ] Scoring fluency: every practice match scored with the official scoresheet / Official Scoring Calculator so scrimmage scores are interpretable; team knows post-match review procedure (referee documents results WITH the team; agreement makes it official; head referee is final).
- [ ] Field ownership: team can reset the full field to wireframe-exact starting state quickly using the printed Field Setup Guide, and knows to check the tournament field and alert the referee to problems (including missing decorative elements) before each match.
- [ ] Gracious Professionalism ready: team knows referees score GP at EVERY match (2/3/4, default 3, added to the Core Values score), including how to earn it even if the robot breaks; Coopetition behaviors prepared, including the cooperative M07/M05 cross-table bonus.
- [ ] Judging session ready in case the scrimmage offers judging: 5-minute innovation project presentation and 5-minute robot design explanation (delivered without the field) rehearsed timed per the judging session flowchart, every member with a part and every member's contribution described, prototype/visuals/scripts packed, rubric criteria (including those feeding Core Values) reviewed.
- [ ] Logistics packed: backed-up programs, charged/updated controller, spare batteries or charged power pack, microSD, one sheet of program-notes paper per home area, scoresheets/calculator, printed Field Setup Guide, and the full packing checklist executed on Nov 5; latest Challenge Updates read and strategy re-verified against them.

## Event-day shape

1. Arrive, pit/table setup, robot + attachment check
2. Practice time on the competition table (sidewalls — different from floor practice!)
3. **Three 2.5-minute robot game matches** — referees score missions and Gracious Professionalism every match
4. Judging session (if offered): **5-minute innovation project presentation + 5-minute robot design explanation**, every team member participating
5. Awards/celebration — cheer for every team

The final rehearsal meetings before Nov 8 run full timed matches from the launch area with the official scoresheet, plus timed run-throughs of both judging presentations.
